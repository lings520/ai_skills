# HTML research reports

Use this skill when producing a research report whose requested deliverable is HTML. Follow any user-specified audience, scope, source, citation, style, and output requirements over these defaults.

## Research integrity

- Define the research question, scope, audience, date range, and deliverable before researching. Ask only material clarifying questions; otherwise state key assumptions and proceed.
- Prefer primary, authoritative, and recent sources appropriate to the subject. Do not invent sources, quotations, statistics, citations, or research findings.
- Check important facts, dates, figures, units, percentages, totals, and comparisons. Distinguish sourced facts from estimates, assumptions, interpretations, and recommendations.
- Cite claims near where they appear. Use numbered footnotes for supporting details or methodological notes and include a references section with complete, usable source details and links where available.
- Ensure every reference is actually used and every material externally sourced claim is traceable to a reference. Clearly label limitations, uncertainty, conflicting evidence, and unsupported areas.

## Report structure

- Place a linked table of contents near the top, after the title and brief executive summary when one is included. Generate links from stable, unique section IDs.
- Organize the report with a clear hierarchy: title, executive summary or key findings, methods/scope where relevant, main sections, limitations, conclusion or recommendations, footnotes, and references.
- Use bullet points for findings, takeaways, assumptions, risks, and recommendations when they improve scanability. Use prose for connected reasoning and context.
- Lead each major section with its main point. Avoid repetition and do not add sections merely to satisfy a template.

## Figures and plots

- Include figures or plots when they materially demonstrate a trend, distribution, comparison, relationship, or geographic/time pattern relevant to the research question. Do not add decorative or unsupported visuals.
- Choose a chart type that matches the analytical question. Label axes, units, dates, categories, and denominators; use honest scales and note transformations or exclusions.
- Give each figure a number, descriptive caption, source note, and nearby interpretation. Provide meaningful alt text or an accessible textual summary.
- Ensure visuals are readable in a browser and remain useful on narrower screens. If data or tooling does not support a trustworthy figure, explain that limitation instead of fabricating one.

## HTML quality

- Produce valid, self-contained HTML unless the user requests a different packaging approach. Use semantic elements, accessible headings, readable tables, responsive styling, and sufficient contrast.
- Keep navigation and citations usable: table-of-contents links should work, footnote links should return to their originating text, and external references should open clearly.
- Before delivery, render or inspect the HTML when possible and review content, links, calculations, figure labels, citation coverage, and layout. State any verification that could not be performed.
